===== src/partials/lab.html =====
<div id="view-lab" hidden>
<div class="lab">
  <p class="intro tk">Pick two lights, A and B. Every {filter} becomes a colour under A and, from the real spectra, a true colour under B. A chromatic adaptation transform (CAT) only gets the colour under A plus the white points of both lights, and has to predict the result under B. The gap between its prediction and the spectral truth is the error measured here.</p>
  <details class="panel">
    <summary>How to read this tab</summary>
    <div class="help">
      <p class="tk"><b>What a CAT does.</b> It takes a colour as XYZ, the XYZ of the white under the old light and the XYZ of the white under the new light. It converts to a cone-like space, scales each of the three channels by new white divided by old white, and converts back. That is the idea you described.</p>
      <p class="tk"><b>Why there is an error.</b> The CAT sees three numbers per colour. Two {filters} with different spectra can give the same XYZ under light A and different XYZ under light B. The CAT gives both the same answer, so it cannot be right for both.</p>
      <p class="tk"><b>What "true" means.</b> The spectral result is {curveL} times the spectrum of light B, divided by the Y of white B. It is the colour relative to the white of light B, which is what a fully adapted viewer would see. That is the case a CAT with D = 1 tries to predict.</p>
      <p class="tk"><b>The two views.</b> "One {filter}, all CATs" shows where each CAT lands compared with the true point. "All {filters}, one CAT" draws a whisker from every true point towards where the chosen CAT puts it. Whiskers are magnified so small errors become visible.</p>
    </div>
  </details>
